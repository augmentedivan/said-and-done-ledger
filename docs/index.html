1. Announced vs. deployed — the headline gap#

Primary source: IMF, Unlocking the Potential: AI in Sub-Saharan Africa, Departmental Paper, 13 Jul 2026, team led by Martin Schindler and Andrew Tiffin. Page numbers below are printed page numbers, verified page by page.**

The unit problem, stated first#

Africa's compute is quoted in three different units, and they tell three different stories. Quote the wrong one and the continent looks four to nine times better supplied than it is.

Source: IMF departmental paper, pp.15 and 18.

Unit Figure What it actually measures
Facility count ~160 or 213 — see Unresolved Register Buildings. The most generous unit available.
Installed IT capacity ~0.4 GW, under 1% of global Megawatts. The honest unit.
AI-capable capacity Not separately published The IMF says existing facilities "primarily support telecommunications and general cloud services rather than frontier-scale AI model training or inference" (p.15).

4. National AI strategy status#

Published / adopted (16+): Mauritius (2018 — Africa's first) · Rwanda (2022/23) · Benin (2023) · Senegal (2023) · Côte d'Ivoire (SNIA 2030, 2024) · Ethiopia (2024) · Egypt (2025–30) · Kenya (2025–30) · Zambia (2024–26) · Namibia (2025) · Zimbabwe (2026–30) · Nigeria · Tunisia · Algeria · Ghana · Lesotho

Drafting / under development: DRC (Oct 2025) · Gabon · Congo-Brazzaville · Togo · Uganda · Tanzania (draft 2025) · Mozambique (Dec 2025) · Sierra Leone (2026) · Chad · Botswana

No confirmed standalone strategy: Malawi · Madagascar · Mali · Angola (folded into digital strategy) · Sudan · Libya

South Africa is the notable gap. The draft policy was withdrawn in late April 2026 after it was found to cite fictitious sources. The redrafted policy isn't expected before end-March 2027 (iAfrica, Oct 2026).

Continental: the AU Continental AI Strategy was endorsed July 2024 (Accra) — five focus areas, fifteen action points. A 2025 working paper found 83% of AI funding concentrated in four countries (Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa, Egypt) and governance structures "largely aspirational." It is single-authored and not peer-reviewed.

Ghana, dated targets: by December 2026, a National AI Delivery Framework with milestones to 2035 is finalised and AI capacity-building extends to 50 Digital Champions plus Chief Directors and Directors (MyJoyOnline, 7 Sep). On 1 Oct 2026 the minister announced a central technical review of all public-sector AI acquisitions: MoCDTI coordinates, NITA scopes each against interoperability, data protection, cybersecurity, sustainability and local skills transfer. Next check 22 Dec 2026.

First sub-regional convergence: on 6 July 2026, Benin, Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Guinea, Mali and Senegal adopted shared AI governance guidelines led by Niyel (iAfrica).

Ministries and laws: on 16 Oct 2026 Morocco's king appointed Ahmed Rahhou Minister of Artificial Intelligence, Digital Transformation and Administrative Reform (TechReview Africa). In Kenya, a Senate committee recommended in October 2026 that the AI Bill 2026 not proceed, over penalties it said could stifle local innovation (Techweez).

H1 2026: US$1.44bn across 146 deals vs 252 a year earlier (TechCabal Insights). One deal (Spiro, US$215m) pushed the half past prior pace. 63 M&A transactions vs 33. This is consolidation, not expansion.

Q1–Q3 2026: US$2.16bn, about 4% below a year earlier. Equity rose 23% to about US$1.5bn while debt fell 33% to US$669m, and the number of startups raising US$100k or more fell 18%, to 311 (Africa: The Big Deal, 20 Oct 2026, via IntelliNews).

Dedicated AI venture capital: Enza Capital's AI Africa Fund reached a US$21m first close in November 2026, on a US$60m target with an US$80m hard cap (NextBillion; Fund Momentum). It is unrelated to the US$60bn Africa AI Fund in §1.

Angels. ABAN: 5,000+ investors across 60+ networks in 37 countries; US$35m into 1,200+ startups over a decade (TechCabal). In 2025, responding groups deployed US$4.4m — with over 90% of individual angels writing cheques below US$25,000, up from 76% in 2024 (ABAN 2025 survey). More deals, smaller cheques.

The structural warning. Partech observed a shift toward later-stage deployment while early-stage stays constrained; Norrsken22's Lexi Novitske said secondaries "could be something that we have to look at" (Semafor). Launch Africa: "If nobody writes that cheque in 2026, there's no Series A class in 2029" (TechCabal).

The benchmark exit: InstaDeep → BioNTech, 2023. Upfront ~£362m cash and shares plus up to ~£200m milestones; Bloomberg valued it at as much as £562m (US$684m) (Bloomberg). Still the only nine-figure African AI exit.

Sources: Africa: The Big Deal, Jan 2026 and Oct 2026; TechCabal Insights; Disrupt Africa, Jul 2026.

Metric Value
Female-CEO startups, Q1–Q3 2026 5.4% of funding (US$115m), double the 2.7% of Q1–Q3 2025; about 2% without one large deal (The Big Deal)

What holds: representation is climbing (19.2% with a female co-founder) while the capital share stays low: 2.2% to female CEOs in 2025, and about 2% in Q1–Q3 2026 once one large deal is set aside (5.4% with it). The problem is depth of cheque, not presence of founders. Correctives: Janngo Capital (Fatoumata Bâ); Madica, which set out to build a portfolio with at least 50% gender diversity in leadership teams (Madica).

8. The counterweight — what the optimism has to answer#

The IMF's estimate (Unlocking the Potential: AI in Sub-Saharan Africa, 21 Jul 2026):

Source: IMF; summary via IMF news.

Scenario Productivity Cumulative GDP over a decade
Current conditions 0.2% ~0.4%
High adoption 2.1% ~4%

Lead author Martin Schindler called the current-conditions figure "little more than a rounding error." Binding constraints: electricity (nearly half the region lacks reliable access), connectivity (38% internet usage in 2024), skills (fewer than a quarter of higher-ed students in STEM; ~9% tertiary enrolment). Sub-Saharan Africa ranks lowest on the IMF's AI Preparedness Index.

The dependency critique. Rest of World (May 2026) documented how far African open-source AI initiatives depend on Meta grants and Google Cloud. On the Anthropic–Rwanda MoU specifically, Ayantola Alayande (Global Center on AI Governance) called it "about adoption, really, rather than building local capacity" and "a nice way to put market capture", warning of vendor lock-in (Devex, Mar 2026).

The deployment critique. Stephen Deng (DFS Lab), responding to the IMF paper: calling for a revolution in long-tail AI use while naming venture capital as a core funder, without addressing ability and willingness to pay, "is simply promoting the more efficient distribution of fragility" (Deng on X; TechCabal Insights).

9. Origins — the history behind the numbers#

Everything above is 2025–26 data. None of it started then. Full account: The Foundations of African AI.

Source: The Foundations of African AI, which links each event.

Era Years What happened
The Wire 1994–2009 First cellular networks (South Africa, 1994); the SAT-3/WASC (2001) and SEACOM (2009) submarine cables end the satellite-only bottleneck on the west and east coasts
The Signal 2003–2011 AIMS, FrontlineSMS, M-Pesa, Ushahidi, iHub, CcHub, Carnegie Mellon Africa: the tools, capital rails and communities built once connectivity existed
The Naming 2013–2019 IBM Research Africa, Data Science Africa, Deep Learning Indaba, AMMI, Google AI Ghana, Microsoft's Africa Development Centre: the field gets AI-specific institutions and starts using the label

Why it's in the Ledger: none of the capital, compute or startup activity tracked above is a green-field event. It's the newest layer on three decades of mostly self-funded infrastructure that predates the 2025 pledge cycle by up to thirty-one years (SAT-3/WASC, 2001, vs. the Kigali declaration, 2025).
